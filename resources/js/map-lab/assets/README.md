# Basic terrain atlas

`terrain-atlas-v1.png` was generated with the built-in image generation tool for the map laboratory. It is a 1536 × 1024 raster atlas: three columns and two rows, 512 × 512 per swatch. Row one: plains, forest, hills. Row two: mountain, lake, ocean. The renderer crops each swatch into a cached hex sprite. The atlas itself has no transition tiles or infrastructure. `tiles.js` now also builds neighbor-aware terrain blends and shoreline bands from these same swatches at runtime, with world-aligned texture sampling and a bounded sprite cache.

Polar extension: snow, tundra, and sea-ice materials are drawn in Canvas by `tiles.js` and share the atlas materials' blending/cache pipeline. Sea ice uses a blue base with pale fractures so polar water remains distinct from white snow-covered land. They are code-native surfaces, not additional AI-generated bitmap assets. The original atlas is unchanged.

## Generation prompt

Use case: stylized-concept. Asset type: ONE game terrain texture atlas, landscape 3:2 aspect ratio, exactly 3 columns by 2 rows of equal square texture swatches covering the full image. No margins, gutters, labels, borders, hex outlines, text or watermark. Each square entirely filled with its terrain, distinct hard cuts exactly at one third and two thirds width and halfway height. Top row left to right: muted olive green grassland with tiny tufts; dense dark green woodland canopy viewed directly overhead with many small readable tree crowns; rolling ochre green hills with gentle shaded ridges. Bottom row left to right: slate gray craggy mountain ridges with pale stone highlights; turquoise blue freshwater lake surface with gentle ripples, WATER ONLY no shoreline; deep navy teal ocean surface with subtle waves, WATER ONLY. Hand-painted strategy game cartography, restrained natural colors, consistent directly overhead orthographic view and soft northwest light. Basic repeatable terrain fills to be cropped inside hex tiles by code, readable at small scale, no roads, bridges, buildings, rivers, shore transitions or objects crossing panels. Uniform terrain density to edges. This is a usable raster sprite atlas, not a presentation mockup.
